Lens 1 · the love map

Where gaming's love lives — and what Play can reach

Love, measured: 12.2B hours watched (936 games, Twitch top-1,000, 12 mo) and 46M people streaming them — the one measure that treats a mobile title and a PC exclusive identically, and it predicts money: streaming correlates 0.76 with launch revenue.

On Play = the game's own listing, verified in five countries; cousins and companion apps arbitrated out. Spin-offs like Wild Rift count as Play titles — not as these games; the ladder shows that cut. Play also thins with depth — on 23% of the top-100 games, 14% below rank 300. And yes, Twitch skews PC — that's part of the finding: wherever streaming is frictionless, Play is absent. And the measure changes the story: hours 17.7%, creators 26.7%.

Lens 2 · funnel vs bucket

The world's biggest funnel. The industry's leakiest bucket.

Love predicts money — so count the money. Play: 83% of store downloads · ≈15% of the world's game spend. Per download: Play $0.71 · App Store $6.73 · Steam $13.65. On mobile, 1% of publishers take 92.5% of the revenue; on Steam, indies take a quarter. One store has a middle class. Counting in-game spend, Steam ≈ 40% of the $43.6B PC market; Epic $0.4B third-party; China's Android stores are most of the ≈$31B "other Android" slice. Hover a store to track it.

Lens 3 · made where, played where

Money crosses oceans — in opposite directions on PC and mobile

Same chart, twice — both real. On PC (ours: Steam top-1,000, lifetime), the East pays the West 2:1. On mobile (Sensor Tower: App Store + Google Play, trailing-year gross), it inverts — the West pays the East 3:1, and East Asia's $30.1B home cell is the largest single flow in gaming — and roughly a third of this table ($30B of $82B, same vendor) moves through Play's own till. The windows differ (lifetime vs annual); the directions and ratios are robust across both. Hover a row to isolate it. Mobile total here ($82B, Sensor Tower) vs Lens 4's $76.7B (AppMagic): same market, two vendors — each slide keeps its own.

Lens 4 · new blood

On PC, new games power the market. On mobile, they barely dent it.

72% of Steam's launched 2025+ · 18% of Play's

All 10 of 10 of Steam's top earners launched in 2025–26; 0 of 10 of mobile's annual top grossers did — Honor of Kings (2015) out-grossed all ten of 2025's biggest launches combined. The top-10 newcomers alone take 17% of Steam, ≈3% of mobile. A store where new games can't climb can't mint evergreens. Steam: our Gamalytic panel (premium; CS2's F2P excluded; incl. re-releases). Play: today's US grossing chart, measured by us — a recency-leaning chart; if anything it flatters Play here. $76.7B = AppMagic gross, both stores — China's other Android stores (~$31B) are most of the gap to Lens 2's $113B.

The position: the funnel is unmatched — and the love is reachable. 59% of it already belongs to publishers Play knows, and when the real game shows up, the hours follow — Fortnite, March 2026.
