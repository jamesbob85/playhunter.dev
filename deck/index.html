The diversity of the games industry · Google Play's position

"Love" here is measured: 12.2 billion hours the world chose to watch — the 936 games among Twitch's top 1,000 categories, past 12 months — and the people who stream them. Why this proxy: watch time is where gaming's conversation happens, streaming correlates 0.76 with launch performance, and publishers put their money where the attention is — which is how attention ends up shaping the catalog itself.

Love lives where Play isn't — but 59% of it comes from companies Play already knows.

The tail of love is enormous, and Play-sized.

The money is young but the love is old — and Play's evergreen shelf is thin.

The funnel is unmatched, and the bucket leaks.

And the biggest pool of money is behind a wall.

Lens 2 · old love

The money is young. The love is old — and it stays old.

2024 · evergreens 48% of watch hours · Play reaches 16.8%

Step through the windows — click or press ↑↓. In every window we can measure, games eight-plus years old hold about half the world's watch hours; new releases never crack a quarter — that's why publishers keep buying the old and rarely bet on the new. And Play's reachable share is creeping up, 16.8% → 18.7%: full ports move it. Play reaches 23% of evergreen love but only 8% of the newest.

Lens 4 · funnel vs bucket

The world's biggest funnel. The industry's leakiest bucket.

Play: 83% of store downloads · ≈15% of the world's game spend. Per download: Play $0.71 · App Store $6.73 · Steam $13.65. On mobile, 1% of publishers take 92.5% of the revenue; on Steam, indies take a quarter. One store has a middle class. Counting in-game spend, Steam ≈ 40% of the $43B PC market; Epic $0.4B third-party; China's Android stores are the unlabeled ~$31B of the mobile segment. Hover a store to track it.

The position

Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, surprises

Strengths

42B downloads a year. Nothing else comes close.

Where a mobile SKU exists, Play usually has it: 131 of 162 mobile-class titles.

In emerging markets, Play is often the only store.

Weaknesses

82% of watch hours aren't on Play — 71% sit on PC-first titles, another 9% console-only. Valve's own two games are 10% by themselves.

86% of Play's watch hours come from a handful of 5+ year-old titles — a narrow legacy shelf, not an evergreen pipeline.

$0.71 per download to the App Store's $6.73 — a 9.5× gap, and no middle class.

Surprises

Hours say Play has 18% of the love. Creators say 27%. The measure changes the story.

Half of all watch hours go to games 8+ years old. The #1 game is 17.

China: 20% of world spend, five of every six dollars staying home, zero Play.

Opportunities

59% of watched love comes from companies already on Play. The games are missing, not the relationships.

8,251 games kept 100+ creators. The tail needs a store, not a spotlight.

1.65M people streamed console titles to under two viewers — through a share button phones don't have.

Love is measurable, and Play's share of it is a choice: when Fortnite returned to Play
in March 2026, its watch hours became reachable overnight. The gap is a choice, not a law.
