Papers, Please is a document-inspection game set at the border checkpoint of Arstotzka, a fictional 1980s communist state. Your job is mechanical: compare papers against regulations, find discrepancies, stamp APPROVED or DENIED. Your problem is that the papers are attached to people — desperate, lying, threatening, bribing people — and your pay depends on throughput while your family depends on your pay.

It's one of the most acclaimed indie games ever made, and its genius is that the mechanics are the message: the rulebook thickens, the queue lengthens, and your empathy quietly becomes a line item you can't afford.

On a touchscreen — dragging documents across a desk, snapping the stamp down — it feels like the format it was always meant for. **$4.99, complete.** Glory to Arstotzka.
