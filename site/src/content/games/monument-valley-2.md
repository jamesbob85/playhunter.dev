Monument Valley 2 is a perspective-puzzle game set inside impossible architecture. You rotate towers, slide platforms, and exploit optical illusions to walk a mother and her child through geometry that could not exist — each chapter a self-contained sculpture in a palette most games would kill for.

The first Monument Valley proved a phone game could be an object of beauty. The sequel is looser, warmer, and quietly about parenthood — teaching a child to walk paths you can't walk for them. It says this in maybe two hundred words across two hours and still lands it.

It's **$3.99, complete, no ads, nothing to unlock** — the model PlayStation buyers take for granted, applied to something you'll finish in two evenings and think about for longer.
